# Track A — Interview Kit (lean version)

**Date:** 2026-06-07
**Time budget:** small. Aim for ~8-12 short chats (15 min each), not formal interviews.

## What I'm trying to learn
Not "is halal a problem" (many of these cooks already know halal cold). The real question:

> **Who lights up at the idea of cooking new dishes from other countries, and what would make a site like this part of their routine?**

I'm hunting for the **explorer** demographic, the people who *enjoy experimenting*, and what they have in common (age, life stage, how they already cook).

## Who to talk to (mix of ages + cooking habits)
- Friends and family who cook
- UCL Islamic Society / MSA, other university societies
- A halal cooking Facebook or WhatsApp group I'm already in
- Mosque / community contacts
- Anyone who follows Muslim food creators

Try to get a spread: some adventurous cooks, some weeknight-staples cooks. The contrast is the insight.

## The rules (so I get truth, not politeness)
- Ask about their **real past behaviour**, not hypotheticals.
- Don't pitch the site. Don't describe my idea until the very end, if at all.
- Shut up and listen. Let silences sit.
- Dig into specifics: "when was the last time…", "what did you do then…"

## The 6 questions
1. Tell me about the last time you cooked something from a cuisine you don't usually make. What made you try it, and how did it go?
2. When you want to try a new recipe, where do you actually look?
3. What's the last recipe that let you down, that didn't turn out how you hoped? What happened?
4. How do you decide an online recipe is worth trusting enough to actually cook it?
5. When you cook food from another country, does the halal side ever slow you down? Walk me through the last time it came up.
6. Is there a recipe site, app, or person you keep going back to? What keeps you coming back?

## What to listen for (the signals)
- **Explorer energy:** do they light up at Q1, or shrug? Note who the experimenters are and what they share.
- **The quality moat:** at Q3, how much does a failed recipe annoy them? "Recipes that actually work" is my edge, so this matters.
- **Trust signals:** at Q4, what makes them trust a source? (Photos? A real person? Reviews? It being someone they know?)
- **Repeat behaviour:** at Q6, what makes something sticky for them.
- **Halal friction:** at Q5, real for them or a non-issue? Either answer is useful.

## Outreach templates

**Casual (friend / WhatsApp):**
> Hey! I'm working on a little halal recipe project and I'm trying to understand how people actually find and choose recipes. Could I steal 15 mins to ask you about how you cook? No prep needed, just your honest habits. Would really help me.

**Group / DM:**
> Hi! I'm a home cook building a halal recipe site and doing a bit of research. I'd love 15 minutes with anyone who enjoys trying recipes from different cuisines, just to hear how you find and pick what to cook. Totally casual, and I'd be really grateful. Drop me a message if you're up for it!

## After ~8-12 chats
Look for the pattern: who are the explorers, what do they share, and what would make a beautifully-made halal world-cooking site stick for them. That, plus retention data (Track C), is the go/no-go signal.
