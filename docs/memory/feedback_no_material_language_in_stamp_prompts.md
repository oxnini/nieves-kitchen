Stamp prompts must NOT describe motifs as woven, embroidered, ceramic, tapestry, fabric, cloth, pottery, painted, glazed, or any other physical material. Even when the *vocabulary* for a motif is drawn from a textile or ceramic tradition (tocapu, Marajoara, kente, ikat, etc.), the prompt must describe it as a flat ink-line geometric stamp on transparent background — consistent with the ink-impression-on-transparent grammar already documented in `project_stamp_aesthetics_strategy.md`.

**Why:** User reinforced 2026-05-16 that stamps must look like real rubber-stamp impressions — when applied, the parchment of the passport page should show through the transparent negative space. Words like "woven panel" or "ceramic glyph" suggest the stamp depicts a material object, breaking that illusion. The cultural source culture inspires the *shape* of the motif, not the material being depicted.

**How to apply:**
- Describe corner/border motifs as: "small ink-line geometric panels in [Quechua tocapu / Marajoara stepped / Maroon Asafo] vocabulary" — the heritage names the *style*, not the substrate.
- Always reaffirm "flat line-art, no fills, transparent interior" alongside heritage-vocabulary references.
- Never write phrases like "woven tocapu panel", "ceramic glyph", "tapestry-style border", "ngatu cloth motif". Reword to: "tocapu-style geometric ink panel", "Marajoara-derived stepped-diamond line motif", "ngatu-derived geometric strip in flat ink".
- Carry this discipline across all 13 region enrichments, not just Andean.
