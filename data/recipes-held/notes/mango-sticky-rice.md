## Sources
- **Hot Thai Kitchen (Pailin Chongchitnant), "Thai Mango and Sticky Rice"** (fetched in full). The backbone: wash 4 to 5 times, cold soak 4 h to overnight (she says not to use hot-soak or other methods for this dessert, as the rice goes mushy once the syrup goes in); drain until it stops dripping so the bottom does not go soggy; damp (not wet) muslin; leave space around the rice; syrup taken off at the first bubble and never reduced; reheat it to steaming and pour over the rice immediately; cover 20 min, fold bottom to top, another 20 min; salted topping thickened with rice flour; white sugar keeps the rice white; mango ripeness cues (wrinkles, buy a week ahead); colour methods (pandan water, butterfly pea) and the "refrigerate after 4 h if soaking in plant-dyed water" rule; crispy mung bean method; storage.
- **Hot Thai Kitchen, "7 Ways to Make Thai Sticky Rice"** (fetched). How to recognise the rice (long grain, product of Thailand, opaque bright white, not sushi rice), why it cannot be boiled like ordinary rice; the metal-sieve steaming method (water at least 2.5cm below the sieve, do not fill it, clear a hole in the middle); the "sprinkle a little water and steam more" fix for undercooked grains; the tested microwave method (15 min hot soak, 1 : ½ water, 3 min then 2-min bursts, rest 5 min, extra 1 to 2 tbsp water if needed), used for the microwave fork.
- **David Thompson, *Thai Street Food*** (republished recipe). Hot rice into a glass/ceramic bowl the moment it is cooked, coconut stirred through until glossy, covered rest; push the pandan leaf into the rice while it rests; topping of coconut cream with only ½ tsp rice flour per 120ml (why I cut the draft's starch to ½ tsp for coconut cream); cut mango cheeks and slice crosswise.
- **Pok Pok (Andy Ricker / JJ Goode), via Food52** (search excerpt only; page rate-limited). Both coconut mixtures carry more salt than you would expect, which is why the dish tastes less sweet than people expect; topping ratio about 1.5 tbsp sugar + 1.5 tsp kosher salt per 2 cups cream, i.e. about 1 tsp sugar and just under ¼ tsp fine salt per 120ml, which matches the draft's topping exactly.
- **America's Test Kitchen article with Leela Punyaratabandhu** (fetched). Mango quality decides the dish; ripe = gives to gentle pressure, skin wrinkles and spots; 1 h soak absorbs nearly as much as 24 h (reassurance that 4 h is plenty); grains look "swimming" then absorb it all.
- **Rachel Cooks Thai** (Thai-born cook, fetched). Do not use jasmine (gloppy), and Thai sticky rice is not Chinese/Japanese short-grain glutinous rice; do not cook it in coconut milk in a rice cooker; palm sugar darkens it; warm rice with slightly cool mango.
- **Cooking Therapy** and **Hungry in Thailand** (fetched): ratio cross-checks; cheesecloth on a steamer insert; pour sauce immediately.
- **ThaiTable** (fetched): microwave cross-check (3-minute bursts, stir, done when every grain is translucent; white centre = uncooked).

## What makes it not bog-standard
1. **Salt calibrated in both coconut mixtures** (Pok Pok, HTK, cross-checked ratio): ¾ tsp in the rice, ¼ tsp in the topping, plus a "taste it: it should taste like salted caramel" cue so a first-timer does not cut it.
2. **Hot rice into hot (never boiled) coconut, then a 20 + fold + 20 rest** (HTK, Thompson): the moment that decides texture; the fold stops a wet bottom and dry top.
3. **Foolproof steaming for a first-timer** (HTK 7 ways): cold soak, drain until no drips, damp-not-wet cloth, 3cm layer with a hole through the middle, gap round the edge, water 2.5cm below, a real doneness test (translucent, no white dot, chewy, no core) and the sprinkle-of-water fix.
4. **Mango buying and ripening rules** (HTK, Leela via ATK): buy days ahead, ripe cues, UK varieties; optional chilled mango against warm rice (Rachel Cooks Thai).
5. **One tin for everything** substitution: thick cream off the top of an unshaken 400ml tin for the topping, the rest for the rice, so the cook need buy only one tin.

## Decisions I made for the cook
- Sugar 100g (not 80g) and salt ¾ tsp (not ½): see ratio table.
- 3 honey/Alphonso mangoes (or 2 large) instead of the draft's 2: honey and Alphonso mangoes are small (each cheek about 70 to 90g), and 2 of them is thin against 300g raw rice; sources run 2 Thai-size mangoes per 200g rice.
- Kept 300g rice for 4 as drafted (a generous portion: about 75g raw rice each; HTK serves 6 from 200g). Yield says "Serves 4 generously".
- Topping starch ½ tsp for coconut cream (Thompson), 1 tsp if made with coconut milk (HTK).
- Steaming time 25 to 30 minutes (draft said 20 to 25): HTK gives 20 to 25 for 200g and 20 to 30 for a sieve; this is 300g.
- Sesame as the main crunch; mung beans as a variation.
- Storage follows UK rice guidance (leftovers eaten within a day) rather than HTK's 3 days.

- Difficulty Easy: no hard technique, just timing.

### Changes
1. Improvised steamer made safe and unambiguous: metal sieve or colander only (never plastic); hold it against the outside of the pan to see how far it hangs, fill to 2.5cm below that so the rice never touches the water even at a hard boil; use a deeper pan if that leaves under 3cm of water.
2. Cloth corners tucked in so nothing hangs down outside the pan towards a gas flame (fire risk).
3. The halfway water top-up now says how to do it (pour round the edge of a steamer; lift a sieve off with oven gloves onto a plate). Before, it said "check the level" with no way to see it.
4. Steam safety: lift the lid away from you, test grains with a spoon not fingers, oven gloves or tongs for the hot cloth corners; "test again" after the extra 5 minutes.
5. Reheating the sweet coconut was timed "a couple of minutes before the rice is ready", which a first-timer cannot know. Now: when the rice passes the test, turn the heat off, leave it covered so it stays hot, and reheat the coconut for 1 to 2 minutes.
6. Moved the salted topping and sesame from "while the rice soaks" to the 40-minute coconut soak. An overnight soak meant cooked coconut cream sitting out all night (a food-safety problem), and the cook is otherwise idle during that 40 minutes. headnoteInstructions updated to match.
7. Topping consistency cue: "like double cream, coats the spoon, runs off in a slow ribbon"; added a fix if it stays as thin as milk (another ¼ tsp starch slurry, back to a bubble) alongside the existing too-thick fix.
8. Shake or stir the coconut milk tin before measuring, so the 250ml is not mostly thin water or mostly cream.
9. Clear end of the soak: "tip the bowl: a thin slick, not a pool; if a pool, fold again and give it 10 minutes", so it is neither wet nor over-handled.
10. Mango cutting rewritten for a nervous cook: cut the cheeks with the skin on for grip (a peeled mango stood upright is slippery), move the knife out if it hits the stone, scoop each cheek out with a large spoon, then slice. Cut during the second rest.
11. Rinse the ramekin with cold water so the dome of rice turns out cleanly.
12. Headnote: buy the grains, not the glutinous rice flour on the same shelf; coconut cream from a tin or carton, not the hard block of creamed coconut (a common UK mix-up); ripen mangoes in a paper bag, and judge by feel and smell, not colour.
13. Storage: homemade salted topping kept in the fridge "up to 3 days" rather than a week.
