# salt-and-pepper-chicken (m-salt-pepper)

## Sources
- **Gridiron Meat, "Salt & Pepper Chicken (Fakeaway Style)"** (the draft's own source, UK takeaway). Seasoning ratio about 6 salt : 4 sugar : 2 white pepper : 2 chili flakes : 1 five spice. The mix is made in excess and only part of it is used. Air fryer at 200°C for 12 to 15 minutes, shaken halfway. Its Shaoxing marinade was not used (halal).
- **The Woks of Life, Cantonese salt and pepper chicken (and their salt and pepper pork chops, for technique only).** Thighs. Marinade salted lightly, with the finishing salt kept separate and measured: 1 tsp salt, 3/4 tsp white pepper, a pinch of sugar for about 565g. Double fry at 175°C, with at least a 5 minute rest between the fries "so steam escapes". Garlic pulled while still pale ("browned garlic turns bitter"). Aromatics warmed in a dry pan, then the heat turned off before the seasoning goes in. Baking soda in the coating for crispness.
- **Chinese Cooking Demystified, "Chinese Salt and Pepper [Anything]".** Guangdong salt and pepper uses white pepper; elsewhere in China it is Sichuan pepper. Since the marinade is already salted, the finishing mix is measured (about 1¾ tsp per batch) instead of added to taste. Dry dredge applied just before frying. Double fry 145°C then 195°C. The final toss is low or no heat so the seasoning clings without scorching. Toast, grind and sieve Sichuan pepper (used in the variation).
- **Omnivore's Cookbook (Maggie Zhu), Air Fryer Salt and Pepper Chicken.** Spray until no dry cornstarch shows. Single layer, no overlap. 176°C for 10 minutes, flip and re-spray, then 200°C for about 5 minutes. Spice mix: 2 tsp white pepper : 1 tsp salt : 1/2 tsp sugar : 1/8 tsp five spice for 450g, with about half used and the rest to taste.
- **Kitchen Sanctuary, Air Fryer Salt and Pepper Chicken** (search summary only; the page returned 403). Thighs at 200°C for about 20 minutes, shaken twice, with spray oil. Onion and chili cooked separately and tossed back in. Thigh stays juicier than breast.
- **RecipeTin Eats, Crispy Salt and Pepper Squid.** Cornflour gives the crunch and plain flour the golden colour. Baking powder makes the coating lighter. Double fry at 160°C then 200°C. Season lightly at the end because the coating is already salted.
- **Khin's Kitchen.** Potato starch and a double fry. Fry in batches without crowding, and drain on a rack. Ginger slices with the garlic (used in the ginger variation).
- Milk Street and CooksInfo for salt density: flaky Maldon is about 2.5 to 3 tsp per 1 tsp of table salt by weight, which works out to about 2.7g per tsp.

## Draft applied
- Default fork `cook = fry` (deep fry) is the main method. Draft quantities kept: 600g thighs; 1 egg white, 1 tsp soy and 1/2 tsp salt in the marinade for 15 minutes; 80g cornflour plus 20g plain flour; the full seasoning (1.5 tsp flaky salt, 1 tsp sugar, 1 tsp white pepper, 1/2 tsp chili flakes, 1/2 tsp five spice, 1/2 tsp garlic powder, 1/4 tsp optional MSG); onion, green pepper, 2 red chilies, 4 garlic cloves and 4 spring onions; fry at 170°C then 190°C; toss off the heat.
- The air fryer fork (`air`) is written as a full, visible alternative step straight after the second fry, under the heading "Fry twice (or air fry)". It is re-timed to 200°C for 8 minutes, a shake and re-spray, then 6 to 8 minutes more, in two batches, with the first batch returned for the last 2 minutes.
- The oven fork (`oven`) is in variations: 220°C (200°C fan) on a rack, 20 to 25 minutes, turned and re-sprayed halfway.
- No `yourNote` on this dish.
- Un-ticked tweaks: `spicebag` and `chips` are fully written variations. `sichuan` is a variation (toast, grind, sieve, and use in place of half the white pepper). `starch` (potato starch) is a substitution, at the same weight.

## What makes it not bog-standard
1. **A measured seasoning that is right when you use all of it, plus a flaky versus fine salt guard.** The draft's 1.5 tsp of flaky salt weighs about 4g, the same as roughly 3/4 tsp of fine salt. Fine salt at the same spoon measure would more than double the salt. The ingredient line, the headnote and the first tip all say to halve it for fine salt. The total salt works out to about 2g per serving (arithmetic below). Sources: Gridiron (ratio), Woks of Life and CCD (keep the finishing salt separate and measured because the marinade is already salted), Milk Street (salt density).
2. **A coating built for crunch.** Cornflour for the crunch and plain flour for colour (RecipeTin). Half a teaspoon of baking powder for a lighter, bubbly crust (RecipeTin; Woks of Life uses baking soda). The coated chicken rests for 10 minutes, so the flour hydrates and does not flake off or stay powdery in the air fryer.
3. **A real double fry with a rest.** 170°C for 4 to 5 minutes until 74°C, a 5 minute rest on a rack, then 190°C for 1 to 2 minutes. Woks of Life and CCD explain why: the rest moves moisture to the surface and the hot second fry drives it off.
4. **Vegetables and garlic that stay fresh and crunchy.** Onion and pepper are cut into large 2cm pieces and given only 2 minutes in a smoking pan. The garlic is chopped, not crushed, and goes in with the heat turned down, pulled at pale gold (Woks of Life). The toss happens with the heat off, lasts 20 to 30 seconds and has no liquid, and the dish goes onto a wide platter rather than a deep bowl (Woks of Life, CCD). For a small pan, the tip is to toss in a warmed mixing bowl.
5. **The cook's palate.** An optional finish of fresh green chili and coriander, the air fryer as a full alternative, and egg fried rice suggested alongside, since the site already has that recipe.

## Decisions I made for the cook
- Servings 4, with yield text "Serves 3 to 4 with rice". The draft said 3 to 4, and nutrition is worked per quarter.
- Time is 50 minutes active and 70 total. The draft said 40, but cutting, a 15 minute marinade, a 10 minute coating rest, three first-fry batches, the rest and the second fry add up to about 70.
- Garlic is "finely chopped (not crushed)" rather than the draft's "minced". Crushed garlic is wet and burns in a hot wok.
- Spring onions are split: the whites go in with the garlic and the greens at the end. The draft put all of them in at the end.
- 1 litre of frying oil in a 20cm saucepan, 3 to 4cm deep and no more than a third full. Three batches (two in a wide wok).
- Baking powder added (1/2 tsp). It is a small, well-sourced crispness move, not a flavour change.
- Ginger is offered as a variation, not in the main recipe, so the dish stays the takeaway classic. The cook likes ginger with garlic, but the draft does not have it.
- "Pork chops" was removed from the description's history line and replaced with "tofu or mushrooms".
- Coordinates are set to Guangzhou (23.13, 113.26), because the dish descends from Cantonese salt and pepper cooking. Influences are China, then United Kingdom.
- Tags: high-protein, spicy, comfort food, treat (deep fried, like the sibling crispy chili beef). Not quick.
- featuredIngredients is `['chicken']`, since the dish is defined by its chicken.

## Verification
Second cook, 2026-10-27. Backup of the pre-verify file: S/research/before-verify-salt-and-pepper-chicken.ts.

Checked and left as they were: the salt load (about 8g in all, about 2g per serving, roughly 0.85% of the food weight, in line with Woks of Life and Omnivore's measured finishing salt); 1 tsp white pepper for 600g; the double fry at 170°C then 190°C, with three batches and then two in a 20cm pan with 1 litre of oil (about 3.2cm deep, under a third full); the air fryer timing (200°C, 8 minutes plus 6 to 8, so 14 to 16 per batch, against Omnivore's 15 and the draft's 15 to 18); vegetables getting about 2.5 minutes of heat in all; the nutrition, re-estimated at about 430 kcal (within 5%); the dietary flags; and the time (50 active, 70 total).

1. Salt line made unambiguous: "sea salt flakes, measured as whole flakes, about 4g (or ¾ teaspoon of fine salt, never 1½)". The old line said "crumbled fine" before measuring, which reads as crumble-then-measure and packs more salt into the spoon.
2. The headnote and the salt tip now say the same thing: measure the flakes whole, use ¾ teaspoon of fine salt instead, or weigh 4g.
3. Oil depth for the 24cm pot a normal kitchen actually has: 1 litre in that pot is only about 2.2cm deep, so the steps and the ingredient line now say 1.5 litres for 24cm.
4. Deep-fry safety line added: turn the handle in, keep a lid or tray beside the pan, turn the heat off if the oil smokes, smother it if it catches, never use water. The chicken is lowered in with tongs or a slotted spoon, away from you.
5. The coating rest now says to start heating the oil as the rest begins. Before, a cook reading in order would wait 10 minutes and only then spend another 8 to 10 minutes heating the oil.
6. "Instead of the three steps above" changed to "four". There are four deep-fry steps: heat, first fry, rest and second fry.
7. HeadnoteInstructions: the air fryer takes "about 5 minutes longer", not 10 (worked out at about 32 minutes against about 27 for frying).
8. The garlic step tells cooks on an electric hob to lift the pan off for 10 seconds when turning the heat down, because a smoking-hot electric ring would burn the garlic.
9. The second-fry step and the drain tip no longer ban kitchen paper, which contradicted the equipment fallback. They now say uncovered, in one layer.
10. The salt-sensitive line now says to add the held-back seasoning only if a tasted piece needs it.

Image: kept the current one (AAOZNDwsTuY). I looked at about 160 more free Unsplash candidates (S/img/snp3 and S/img/snp4 contact sheets). Every one with peppers or onion was a wet, sauced stir-fry, a Sichuan dried-chili dish with diners' faces, or a battered chicken in glaze. None was a better honest match for dry, seasoned salt and pepper chicken.

FLAGs (unchanged): not over 800 kcal. Label check on the light soy sauce (halal-certified), and on any curry sauce in the spice bag variation. The image is a loose match, with no peppers or onion in it.
