## Sources
- **RecipeTin Eats, Vodka Pasta (penne alla vodka)**: ratio check (500g pasta : 140g tomato paste : 300ml cream : 50g parmesan, so 250g : 70g : 150ml : 25g; the draft's 80g paste / 150ml cream sits right on it); fry the paste until it darkens to take off the sour, tinny edge and even out brands; stock as her alcohol-free swap (rejected here, see below); pasta water's starch emulsifies with the fat so the sauce clings rather than pools; grate parmesan yourself, pre-grated goes grainy; double cream holds best, lighter creams are riskier.
- **Brian Lagerstrom, Pasta Vodka**: Calabrian chili quantity (30 to 40g chopped Calabrian chilies + flakes per 450g pasta, about 1.5 tbsp per 250g), chili cooked in the oil for a couple of minutes before the paste, paste bloomed until the colour deepens, cream simmered 3 to 5 min to thicken, butter (75g per 450g) and parmesan stirred in with the pasta, then adjusted with pasta water. Contributed the butter-plus-cheese finish and the "bloom the chili in the oil first" step.

- **Vodka-substitute round-up (Tasting Table, Food Republic)**: water plus a little lemon juice or a mild vinegar is the common swap for vodka's sharpness. Used for the "brightness" variation and the cider vinegar in the Calabrian substitute.

## What makes it not bog-standard
1. **Pasta goes in when the onion is soft**, so the deglazing water is taken from the pot a few minutes in, already starchy. The sequencing means sauce and pasta land together and the deglaze itself starts the emulsion. (Draft's deglaze idea + RecipeTin/Lagerstrom on starch.)
2. **Less water, lightly salted** (2.5 L, 1 tbsp fine salt for 250g): starchier water for the emulsion, and the water ends up in a sauce already salted by chili paste and cheese. (General pasta-water technique; RecipeTin's note that parmesan adds salt.)
3. **Brick red is a colour cue, not a time**, with smell and the rusty film as cues, and a reason: raw paste is the "flatness" people blame on missing vodka. (RecipeTin, Table of Spice.)
4. **Cold butter and cheese off the heat, with a greasy/grainy rescue** (splash of pasta water, hard toss) and "leave it slightly loose". Addresses the cook's brief that the sauce must never split or go greasy. (Olive, Lagerstrom, RecipeTin.)
5. **A real Calabrian substitute**: flakes + sweet paprika for colour + half a tsp cider vinegar for the tang the jarred chilies are packed in, or harissa (which the cook likes).
