## What makes it not bog-standard
1. **Steep time as the heat dial, with a strain step** (Forked Spoon). Honey and flakes warmed (never boiled) then steeped through the coating and frying; taste between batches and strain at the heat you want so it stops climbing. Gives the cook control and makes the heat repeatable.
2. **Cold butter whisked into warm, not hot, honey** (Cafe Delites). Emulsifies into a glaze that clings. I added the failure cue: if the butter forms a greasy layer, the honey was too hot, cool a minute and keep whisking.
3. **Brine rubbed into the dredge + a 10 to 15 minute rest** (Serious Eats/Food Network method; Weeks and Forked Spoon for the rest). Craggy crust that stays on in the oil. Rest coincides with heating the oil, so it costs no time.
4. **Crust protection at the end**: drain on a rack (Weeks, BBC), salt straight out of the oil, keep warm in a 150°C (130°C fan) oven, and spoon the honey over the tops only at the table, with a third in a jug (Forked Spoon "brush, don't drench"; Weeks "serve immediately").
5. **Oil safety and control** (BBC, Weeks, Cloake): 3cm of oil, no more than a third of the pot, 180°C start, hold 160 to 170°C, clump test without a thermometer, what to do if it smokes, lay pieces away from you, never unattended, and a fallback for thick pieces that brown before 74°C (5 minutes in the warm oven).

## Decisions I made for the cook
- Salt rebalanced: brine 1.5 tsp (draft 1), dredge 1 tsp (draft 2). UK fine salt is about 6g a teaspoon; 2 tsp in 200g of dredge is about 6% salt, roughly three times the Serious Eats ratio, and most of the seasoning should be in the meat. Total salt per serving stays about the same.
- Thighs kept whole (halve any oversized one) rather than cut into strips, as drafted.
- Oil depth 3cm in a heavy pot (about 1.5 litres in a 24cm pot), following Cloake's point that a moderate depth is easier to control, and safer than a full deep-fry at home.
- Honey made first, about an hour before eating, so the steep runs alongside the frying; strained when hot enough.
- Butter is unsalted (pinch of salt is separate) so the salt is controllable.
- Garnish: flaky salt as drafted; sliced green chili optional (the cook's habit); coleslaw and pickles optional on the side. No coriander, to keep it Southern.
