## Sources
- **Just One Cookbook, Yaki Udon.** Blanch frozen udon for only 30 to 40 seconds, just until the strands separate, then drain well, because wet noodles make a watery sauce. Use a large pan so the noodles fry rather than steam. Toss with tongs so they don't break. Pour the soy round the hot side of the pan so it sizzles. Vegetable quantities for 2 come to about 400g (onion 100g, cabbage 227g, carrot 50g, shiitake), and I used that to size the default veg at about 450g.
- **RecipeTin Japan, Yaki Udon.** Add the vegetables in stages, starting with the slowest (carrot first, then cabbage, then spring onion). Cook the protein first and return it with the noodles so it doesn't overcook. Stir-fry until some noodles start to brown, and keep going until the sauce has almost evaporated. Worcestershire is suggested for a yakisoba-style tang, which supports keeping it optional.
- **Chopstick Chronicles, Yaki Udon.** Marinate the sliced meat briefly (soy plus ginger) so the dish doesn't taste flat. Put garlic in the sauce, and the vegetables can vary (it uses green pepper). Parboil the udon briefly and drain thoroughly. Sanuki frozen udon is recommended.
- **RecipeTin Eats, How to Tenderise Beef (velveting).** Slice thinly at 90 degrees to the grain, and only thin slices go into a tenderising marinade. Rump, sirloin and other economical steaks are the right cuts, and there is no point using expensive ones. The bicarbonate method uses 3/4 tsp per 250g and then a rinse. I scaled that down to an optional quarter teaspoon with no rinse, the light-touch restaurant dose, and kept the main recipe on the cornflour, soy, water and oil velvet.
- **Okonomi Kitchen, Yaki Udon.** Use a large wok to reduce clumping, cook the firmest vegetables first, and rinse the noodles to stop a starchy texture. (This one is vegan and uses mentsuyu and mirin, so it contributed technique only.)
- **Marion's Kitchen** didn't come up in search and I didn't chase it, since there were already 4 sources.

## Picks applied
- **Fork `protein`: beef (thin-sliced steak).** The beef is the main protein, sliced across the grain and velveted. Chicken, prawns and tofu went into variations.
- **Note "no Bonito flakes."** Removed completely. Sesame seeds and chili finish the plate instead.
- **Note "can throw in any veggies like sliced carrot, beansprouts, red peppers etc."** Carrot, red pepper and beansprouts are in the default set, with onion, cabbage and spring onion from the draft. headnoteIngredients says the list is a guide (about 450g of whatever you have, hard ones first, soft ones last) and suggests swaps.
- **Note "no need for Worcestershire (optional)."** It is listed as "Worcestershire sauce (optional)", the sauce step says "with the Worcestershire sauce if you are using it", and the description says you won't miss it.
- **Note "add a teaspoon of dark soy sauce."** 1 tsp dark soy sauce is in the sauce. There is a substitution in case you have none.
- **Note "a bit of garlic minced in the sauce."** 2 garlic cloves, finely minced, go into the sauce.
- **Note "toasted sesame seeds at the end."** 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds is in "To finish", with a prep step for toasting raw ones in the dry wok first.
- **Brief: no mirin or sake.** The sauce has none. Sugar replaces mirin's sweetness, and the draft's 1 tsp rice vinegar stays for brightness.
- **Brief: make sure the beef is tender.** The steps use the optional 20-minute freeze for thin slicing, slicing 3mm across the grain, and a cornflour velvet for 10 to 15 minutes. The beef is seared hot in a single layer for 1 minute plus 30 seconds, taken out while still pink, and returned only for the final minute. A tip covers the bicarb option for cheaper cuts.
- **Un-ticked tweaks.** Butter and soy finish, fried egg, gochujang and curry are all in variations.

## Decisions I made for the cook
- **250g of beef for 2 (the draft had 200g).** The title is Beef Yaki Udon, so beef leads, and it is still one wok's worth seared in up to two batches.
- **2 tbsp oil in total (1 for the beef, 1 for the veg), plus 1 tsp in the velvet.** The draft had 1 tbsp, which isn't enough to sear the beef and then fry 450g of veg over high heat.
- **Light soy in the sauce is 1.5 tbsp (the draft had 2).** The dark soy and the soy in the marinade make up the difference, so the salt level is about the same as the draft (roughly 1.4g sodium per serving).
- **Sauce water is 2 tbsp (the draft had 1)** so it coats 400g of udon before it reduces.
- **Shichimi or chili flakes stay, plus an optional fresh green chili** (the cook likes green chili). Pickled red ginger stays as an optional extra with a label tip.
- **Ginger is NOT in the main sauce.** The cook's note says garlic only, so ginger is offered as a variation, since the cook likes ginger with garlic elsewhere.
- **Coordinates are Kokura, Kitakyushu,** where yaki udon is commonly said to have begun. The description says "said to have started in Kitakyushu".
- **featuredIngredients is `beef`** (the title protein).
- **Tags: quick, high-protein (39g, threshold 25), comfort food.**
